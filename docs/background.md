## 1. What a Life Insurance Advice Practice Does

A life insurance advice practice, often called a **risk advice** practice, helps people work out how much cover they need, recommends cover that fits, puts it in place, and helps them claim when something goes wrong. Its advisers give **personal advice** under an Australian financial services (AFS) licence, and all personal advice must meet the best interests duty.

A practice:

- runs the **fact find** and works out the client's **needs**
- researches insurers, usually from an **approved product list (APL)**
- gives the advice in a **Statement of Advice (SoA)**
- takes the **application** through **underwriting** with the insurer
- **reviews** the cover as the client's life changes
- supports clients and families through **claims**, and advocates for them with the insurer

**Who's who.**

| Party | Role | Caldova example (fictional) |
|---|---|---|
| Client | The person advised. On a policy, the **life insured** is the person whose life or health is covered, the **policy owner** owns the policy, a **beneficiary** receives the benefit on death, and a **claimant** makes a claim | Priya, Grace, George and Harpreet, Tom, and Robert |
| Adviser | A relevant provider on ASIC's Financial Advisers Register, who gives the advice and signs the SoA | Amelia, Senior Risk Adviser |
| Licensee | The AFS licence holder responsible for its advisers | Caldova's advice is given under an AFS licence |
| Insurer | A life company that issues policies, underwrites applications and decides claims | Harbourline Life, Corella Life, Lyrebird Life and Northbank Life, on Caldova's APL |
| Super fund trustee | Holds cover inside super and pays insured benefits under the fund's rules | Riverbend Super (an industry fund) and Kestrel Retail Super |
| Referral partner | Refers clients with their consent | Hannah Lewis, mortgage broker (Yarra Home Loans), and Marcus Tan, accountant (Bayside Business Accountants) |

**How the practice is paid.** An adviser can be paid an advice fee, commission from the insurer, or both. Life-risk commission is capped under the Life Insurance Framework (LIF) and, for cover issued from 9 Jul 2025, needs the client's informed consent (see [section 6](#6-key-rules--numbers-fy2026-27)). Caldova charges no advice fee for protection-only advice and is paid by commission within the caps, disclosed in dollars. Its FSG explains why it can't describe itself as independent, impartial or unbiased ([Corporations Act s923A and s942B](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A00818/latest/text)).

### The Cover Caldova Advises On

| Cover | What it pays | Inside super? | Scenarios |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Life cover**, with a terminal illness benefit | A lump sum on death. The terminal illness benefit pays the cover early if the life insured is diagnosed with a terminal illness, as defined in the insurer's PDS | Yes | [1: Priya](scenario-1-family-protection.md), [5: Robert](scenario-5-death-claim.md) |
| **TPD cover** (total and permanent disability) | A lump sum if the life insured is permanently unable to work. **Own occupation** means unable to work in their own job; **any occupation** means unable to work in any job they are reasonably qualified for. When TPD is packaged with life cover, a TPD claim reduces the life cover | Any-occupation TPD only. Own-occupation TPD is held outside super, often super-linked | [1: Priya](scenario-1-family-protection.md), [3: George](scenario-3-business-protection.md) |
| **Trauma cover** (critical illness) | A lump sum on a covered critical illness or serious injury, such as cancer, a heart condition, a major head injury or a stroke. It doesn't cover mental health conditions | No new trauma cover inside super | [1: Priya](scenario-1-family-protection.md), [2: Grace](scenario-2-fraudulent-claim.md) |
| **Income protection** | A monthly benefit while the life insured can't work because of illness or injury, after a **waiting period** (typically 14 days to 2 years) and for up to a **benefit period** (such as 2 or 5 years, or to an age such as 65) | Yes, but premiums are only tax-deductible outside super | [1: Priya](scenario-1-family-protection.md), [4: Tom](scenario-4-income-protection-claim.md) |
| **Business protection** | **Key person** cover replaces what a business loses if a key person dies or is disabled. **Buy-sell** cover funds the transfer of an owner's share under a buy-sell agreement, which a lawyer drafts | Usually outside super | [3: George](scenario-3-business-protection.md) |

Sources: Moneysmart on [life cover](https://moneysmart.gov.au/how-life-insurance-works/life-insurance-cover), [TPD](https://moneysmart.gov.au/how-life-insurance-works/total-and-permanent-disability-tpd-insurance), [trauma](https://moneysmart.gov.au/how-life-insurance-works/trauma-insurance) and [income protection](https://moneysmart.gov.au/how-life-insurance-works/income-protection-insurance); the ATO on [key person cover (IT 155)](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/print?DocID=ITR%2FIT155%2FNAT%2FATO%2F00001) and [buy-sell cover (ATO ID 2003/1189)](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/print?DocID=AID%2FAID20031189%2F00001).

**Premiums.** Insurers set premiums by age, health, job and lifestyle. **Stepped** premiums (Moneysmart now says "variable age-stepped") are recalculated by age at each renewal, so they start lower and rise as the client gets older. **Level** premiums ("variable") start higher, and because changes aren't based on age, they generally rise more slowly. Neither is guaranteed. ([Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/how-life-insurance-works/life-insurance-cover))

## 2. Inside Super or Outside Super

Life cover can be held inside super or outside it. Where cover is held changes who owns it, who pays for it, what's allowed and how benefits are taxed.

| | Inside super | Outside super |
|---|---|---|
| Who owns the policy | The fund's trustee | The client, or their business |
| Who pays | Premiums are deducted from the super balance, which reduces retirement savings over time | The client, from cash flow |
| Cover allowed | Only cover for events that match a condition of release: death, terminal medical condition, permanent incapacity (any-occupation TPD) and temporary incapacity (income protection). No new own-occupation TPD or trauma since 1 Jul 2014 | Any cover, including own-occupation TPD and trauma |
| Who is paid | The trustee pays under the fund's rules and super law | The policy owner, or the nominated beneficiaries on death |
| Tax on premiums | Not deductible to the client | Income protection premiums are deductible. Life and trauma premiums aren't |
| Tax on benefits | A lump-sum death benefit to a dependant (for tax) is tax-free; non-dependants pay tax on the taxable component. TPD lump sums can be taxed before age 60 | Life, TPD and trauma proceeds are generally not taxable. Income protection benefits are assessable income |

Inside super, two sets of rules decide whether cover starts or continues. Under **Protecting Your Super**, a fund can't keep insuring an account that has received nothing for 16 continuous months unless the account holder elects in writing to keep the cover. That is how Priya's old Kestrel Retail Super cover was about to stop in [scenario 1](scenario-1-family-protection.md). Under **Putting Members' Interests First**, a fund can't start default cover for a new account holder under 25, or for an account with a balance under $6,000, unless the person opts in.

**Super-linking**, also called flexi-linking, splits one arrangement between a super-owned policy and a personally owned one. Cover allowed in super (life and any-occupation TPD) sits in the fund. Cover that isn't (own-occupation TPD and trauma) is held personally and linked to it, and claims are assessed against the super-owned part first. Priya's own-occupation TPD extension is super-linked.

Sources: [SIS Regulations reg 4.07D](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F1996B00580/latest/text) · [SIS Act s68AAA](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A04633/latest/text) · [APRA: Protecting Your Super FAQ](https://www.apra.gov.au/protecting-your-super-package-frequently-asked-questions) · [Moneysmart: insurance through super](https://moneysmart.gov.au/how-life-insurance-works/insurance-through-super) · [Moneysmart: life cover](https://moneysmart.gov.au/how-life-insurance-works/life-insurance-cover) · [ATO: income protection](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/income-deductions-offsets-and-records/deductions-you-can-claim/investments-insurance-and-super/income-protection-insurance) · super-linking: [secondary source](https://www.ifa.com.au/affordable-life-insurance-with-flexi-linking)

## 3. The Advice Process

> **Referral or enquiry → Fact find → Needs analysis → Research → Statement of Advice → Authority to Proceed → Application and underwriting → Policy issued → Reviews**

| Step | What happens | Rules that apply | Caldova department | Scenarios |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Referral or enquiry** | A client asks for help, or a referral partner refers them with their consent. The client gets the Financial Services Guide (FSG) | The hawking prohibition (no offers in unsolicited real-time contact). FSG | Onboarding & Requests | [1](scenario-1-family-protection.md), [3](scenario-3-business-protection.md) |
| **Fact find** | Goals, dependants, debts, income, occupation, health basics and existing cover. Privacy and health-information consent, and authority to obtain existing policy details | Best interests duty: identify the client's objectives, financial situation and needs, and make reasonable inquiries | Onboarding & Requests | All |
| **Needs analysis** | How much cover, of what kind, and how to hold it | Best interests duty: base every judgement on the client's relevant circumstances | Paraplanning | [1](scenario-1-family-protection.md), [3](scenario-3-business-protection.md) |
| **Research** | Compare the APL insurers on definitions, features, exclusions and premiums | Best interests duty: a reasonable investigation of the products | Insurance Research | [1](scenario-1-family-protection.md), [3](scenario-3-business-protection.md) |
| **Statement of Advice** | The advice in writing: scope, strategy, ownership, why it is appropriate, any replacement, and premiums and commission in dollars | SoA content, replacement advice and commission consent | Paraplanning, signed by Amelia | [1](scenario-1-family-protection.md), [3](scenario-3-business-protection.md) |
| **Authority to Proceed** | The client tells the adviser to go ahead | Practice step | Paraplanning | [1](scenario-1-family-protection.md), [3](scenario-3-business-protection.md) |
| **Application and underwriting** | The client answers the insurer's questions. The insurer may ask for medical and financial evidence, then accepts at standard rates, accepts with a loading or an exclusion, or declines | The duty to take reasonable care. Genetic information is protected | Underwriting & New Business | [1](scenario-1-family-protection.md), [3](scenario-3-business-protection.md) |
| **Policy issued** | The policy schedule and a welcome letter. The cooling-off period runs | 14-day cooling-off period | Underwriting & New Business, Client Communications | [1](scenario-1-family-protection.md), [3](scenario-3-business-protection.md) |
| **Reviews** | Regular reviews as life changes. Further advice can be recorded in a Record of Advice (RoA) when the client's circumstances haven't changed significantly | RoA rules | Onboarding & Requests, Paraplanning | [2](scenario-2-fraudulent-claim.md) (Grace's annual review) |

**Underwriting outcomes.** The insurer can accept the application at **standard rates**, with a **loading** (a higher premium, like George's 25%), with an **exclusion** (a condition the policy won't cover, like Priya's back and spine exclusion on income protection), or **decline** it. Accepting a loading, an exclusion or a decline is the client's decision with the adviser.

## 4. The Claims Process and the Adviser's Role

> **Claim notification → Policy and evidence check → Claim pack → Approval and lodgement → Insurer's assessment → Decision → Payment and follow-up**

| Step | What happens | The insurer's obligations under the Life Code | Caldova department |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Notification** | The client, the family or a support person tells Caldova about the claim | Within 10 business days of receiving a claim, the insurer explains the Code, the cover, waiting periods, benefits and the process (cl 5.5). Support persons are recognised (cl 6.13) | Onboarding & Requests |
| **Policy and evidence check** | The definition, waiting period, exclusions, who is paid, and the evidence | | Claims |
| **Approval and lodgement** | A person at Caldova approves before any claim is lodged | | Claims, Team Leader Office |
| **Assessment** | The insurer gathers what it needs | Reply to information requests within 10 business days (cl 5.7). Updates at least every 20 business days (cl 5.6). Surveillance limited to 4 months (cl 5.42(h)) | Claims |
| **Decision** | The insurer accepts, partially accepts or declines | Income-related claims within 2 months of receiving the claim, or of the end of the waiting period if later. Lump-sum claims within 6 months. A written decision within 15 business days of having all the information (cl 5.48–5.50) | Claims |
| **Payment** | The benefit is paid | Income protection benefits paid by the due date, or within 5 business days of all information (cl 5.63). For accepted lump sums of $25,000 or more, the insurer suggests independent financial advice (cl 5.61) | Claims, Client Communications |
| **If the client disagrees** | A complaint to the insurer, then AFCA | A final written response within 30 calendar days (cl 7.13) | Client Communications, Team Leader Office |

Source: [Life Insurance Code of Practice (CALI, effective 1 Mar 2025)](https://cali.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/CALI-003-LICOP-Life-Code-2025-v4-WEB_01.pdf)

**The adviser's advocacy role.** The Life Code binds the insurers that subscribe to it, not advisers (cl 1.10). But at claim time the adviser is often the client's best help: they know the policy, build the claim pack, chase the insurer against its own timeframes, explain decisions in plain English, look for other cover such as cover in super, and support a complaint if the outcome looks wrong. APRA's data suggest it matters. For individual cover in the year to 30 Jun 2026, TPD claims were admitted 81.1% of the time when advised against 71.1% when not, and disability income claims 94.3% against 85.0% ([APRA](https://www.apra.gov.au/news-and-publications/life-insurance-claims-and-disputes-statistics)).

## 5. The Request Lifecycle Inside the Practice

Every request moves through the same five steps, shown on the "One process for both sides" board in the Caldova office. A request might be new advice, a review, a change or a claim.

| Step | Question it answers | Caldova departments | Typical checks and controls |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Discover** | What does the client need, and who is asking? | Onboarding & Requests | Capture the fact find into the Client Case, classify the request, set its priority and flag vulnerable clients |
| **2. Verify** | Is it in good order, and is it really the client? | Onboarding & Requests, Financial Crime | The in-good-order checklist, ID and KYC, document authenticity, and a call-back on the verified number on file |
| **3. Advise** | How much cover, held how, and with which insurer? | Paraplanning, Insurance Research | The needs analysis, the ownership structure, research across the APL, and the SoA drafted for Amelia, who gives the advice and signs it |
| **4. Implement** | Is the policy issued, or the claim lodged, correctly? | Underwriting & New Business, Claims | The duty to take reasonable care, underwriting requirements and decisions the client accepts; the policy and evidence check, the benefit calculation and approval before lodgement |
| **5. Confirm** | Does everyone know what happened and what comes next? | Client Communications | Plain-English letters, referral-partner updates within the client's consent, required disclosures and complaint rights |

The **Team Leader Office** oversees all five steps: the pipeline and service levels, underwriting and claim clocks, complaint (IDR) clocks, approvals, second reviews of advice with total cover of $2,000,000 or more, file quality and vulnerable clients.

**Why "in good order" matters.** A request is in good order when everything needed is there: the FSG, signed privacy and health-information consents, ID, the authority to obtain existing policy details, commission consent, and any support-person authority. When something is missing, the work stops while the practice chases it. Getting requests right the first time is one of the biggest levers in an advice practice, and it is where AI extraction and checklists help most.

These are the settings the agents use, current at 23 Nov 2026. All amounts are in Australian dollars.

|---|---|---|

## 7. Financial Crime Patterns in Life Insurance

The patterns that Caldova's *Guide — financial crime red flags and controls for life insurance* watches for:

- **Identity takeover before a claim.** Someone impersonates a client, changes the contact details, then claims or asks for the benefit to be paid somewhere new. The tell-tale signs are a recent contact change with weak verification, a new device or location, urgency, and a new bank account in another name. This is Grace's story in [scenario 2](scenario-2-fraudulent-claim.md).
- **Fabricated or altered evidence.** Fake specialist reports, edited dates, pasted letterheads, provider numbers in the wrong format, and tampered IDs and bank statements.
- **Payment redirection.** A change of bank details shortly before a benefit is due.
- **Pressure on vulnerable clients.** A third party pushing for a change of ownership, beneficiaries or bank details. That is why every such change needs a person's approval.
- **Misrepresentation at application.** Most non-disclosure is a mistake, not fraud, and the law treats the two differently: an insurer can avoid a life policy for fraud, but for a non-fraudulent misrepresentation only within three years ([Insurance Contracts Act s29](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A02944/latest/text)). The agents never treat a mistake as fraud and never accuse anyone.
- **Policies with a surrender value.** Issuing a life policy with a minimum surrender value is an AML/CTF designated service for the insurer. Policies with no minimum surrender value fall outside the Act's definition of a life policy ([AML/CTF Act](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2006A00169/latest/text)).

Controls used in the demo:

- call back on the **verified contact details on file**, never the details in the request
- hold a request that follows a recent change of contact details
- human approval for any change to ownership, beneficiaries or bank details
- pay benefits only to the policy owner or a nominated beneficiary, under the policy terms
- check the authenticity of every document on a claim
- refer suspected fraud to the insurer's fraud team, with a person's approval
- never accuse the client, who may be the victim

These controls are practice, not regulator text.

## 9. Presenter Notes: Why This Matters for Advice Practices, Licensees and Life Insurers

> **For presenters only.** This is the only place in the demo docs where real organisations are named. The demo itself always uses the fictional **Caldova Life Advice** and its fictional insurers. Never put a real insurer's, licensee's or practice's name, logo, products or address in the UI. Keep any mention factual and neutral.

### Who They Are

- **Advice practices and advisers.** There are about **15,225** current advisers on ASIC's Financial Advisers Register (a count derived from the [register extract](https://data.gov.au/data/dataset/f2b7c2c1-f4ef-4ae9-aba5-45c19e4d3038) as at 19 Nov 2026). Padua WealthData counted 15,012 in July 2026, down from about 24,500 in July 2018 ([Padua WealthData](https://wealthdata.com.au/blog-1/financial-adviser-market-report-2026), secondary). About 13,047 of the current advisers (85.7%) are authorised to advise on life-risk products, but few specialise: CALI counts fewer than 600 advisers who focus on life insurance, and just 185 pure risk advisers ([CALI Safety Net 2025](https://cali.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/CALI_SafetyNet_2025_051125_FA_Digital_web.pdf)).
- **Licensees.** AFS licensees authorise and supervise advisers. They keep the register up to date, keep advice records for 7 years, and fund the CSLR: **$1,312** per adviser for FY2026-27 ([ASIC](https://asic.gov.au/about-asic/dealing-with-asic/compensation-scheme-of-last-resort/cslr-annual-levy)), plus a **$648** special levy per adviser ([ASIC](https://asic.gov.au/about-asic/dealing-with-asic/compensation-scheme-of-last-resort/cslr-special-levy)).
- **Life insurers.** The Life Code has 19 subscribers, insurers and reinsurers, represented by CALI. Individually advised cover in force at 30 Jun 2026 includes death cover on 1.262 million lives ($3.08bn of annual premium), TPD on 0.896 million ($1.84bn), trauma on 0.662 million ($1.54bn) and disability income on 0.666 million ($3.24bn): about $9.7bn across the four ([APRA](https://www.apra.gov.au/news-and-publications/life-insurance-claims-and-disputes-statistics)). CALI reports $13.4bn of claims paid to more than 95,000 people ([CALI Safety Net 2025](https://cali.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/CALI_SafetyNet_2025_051125_FA_Digital_web.pdf)).

### Where They Are with AI

- **Consumers are already asking AI.** CALI's Life Insurance Sentiment Tracker found the rate of AI users receiving financial advice rose 80% among life insurance advice recipients, to nearly one in three. Yet 55% of working Australians say speaking with a real person is very important, and almost half had considered or sought advice about life insurance, but only 12% received it. CALI's warning: an AI agent "can give you an answer in seconds, the consequences of getting it wrong last for decades" ([CALI, 18 Nov 2026](https://cali.org.au/surge-in-ai-advice-raises-alarms-as-cali-presses-government-for-reform/)).
- **Regulators expect governance to keep up.** ASIC's REP 798, *Beware the gap: Governance arrangements in the face of AI innovation* (29 Oct 2024), reviewed how 23 AFS and credit licensees use and plan to use AI, how they identify and mitigate the risks to consumers, and how they govern it ([ASIC](https://asic.gov.au/regulatory-resources/find-a-document/reports/rep-798-beware-the-gap-governance-arrangements-in-the-face-of-ai-innovation/)).
- **Policy is moving, but it isn't law.** The Government's August 2026 package proposes a New Class of Adviser for life insurers and super funds, SoA simplification, and removing the broadest best-interests step ([Treasury](https://treasury.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-08/p2026-797028-npc-fact-sheet.docx)). CALI has welcomed the New Class of Adviser.

**How to position the demo.** *Advice Team in a Day* isn't an AI adviser. It shows AI working **inside** an advice practice, under a human adviser. The agents do the fact finding, checking, calculating, comparing and drafting; Amelia gives the advice and signs it; and people approve every decision that matters. That speaks directly to CALI's point that "a real person matters when the questions are personal", and to ASIC's governance gap: every agent's prompt, tools, citations and output are visible, and the approval gates are explicit.

### Governance Context: Handle with Care

- **The Life Code is changing.** The independent review's final report (30 Jun 2026) made 85 recommendations, and CALI's action plan (30 Sep 2026) supports most of them. A new Code text hasn't been issued, so proposals such as claim-reopening timeframes aren't in force.
- **The LIF commission instrument** is due to sunset on 1 Oct 2027 unless it is remade. No remake has been announced that we could find.
- **Advice reform (Tranche 2)** isn't law. SoAs, RoAs and the best-interests safe harbour are unchanged today.
- **Proposed:** narrowing the advisers' hawking exemption to existing clients, and a review of the Code of Ethics.

Use these as context. Don't build the story around pending reform.

### Don't Overstate

- **AI advice.** The agents never give personal advice. Don't describe the demo as "AI advice": Amelia gives the advice.
- **Regulatory endorsement.** Nothing in the demo has been reviewed or approved by a regulator. It is a concept.
- **Proposals as law.** Tranche 2, the New Class of Adviser and the narrower hawking exemption are proposals.
- **The Life Code and advisers.** The Code binds subscribing insurers, not advisers. Ben's agent tracks the insurer's obligations; it doesn't impose them on Caldova.
- **AML/CTF.** Don't say a risk-only practice is, or isn't, a reporting entity. It depends on what the practice arranges.
- **Income protection.** Don't say APRA requires a 5-year contract term (that measure is suspended) or a switch from own-occupation to any-occupation (there is no such rule).
- **Key person tax.** IT 155 is long-standing, but its currency isn't confirmed. Defer to the client's accountant.
- **AFCA's limits** here come from secondary sources. Check AFCA's own site before quoting them.
- **Fictional numbers.** The insurers, premiums, definitions and policy terms in the scenarios are fictional. Don't compare them with real insurers' rates.
- **Named organisations' AI work.** Don't attribute AI programs to named insurers, licensees or practices without a public source.
